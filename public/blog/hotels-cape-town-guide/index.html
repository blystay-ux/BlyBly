Hotels in Cape Town: The Complete Guide to Where to Stay in 2026

By BLY. August 2026 8 min read

Cape Town is one of the most searched destinations in South Africa — and for good reason. Whether you're heading to the Atlantic Seaboard, the Winelands, or the CBD, finding the right place to stay makes the whole trip. Here's how to navigate it.

Why Cape Town is Different from Every Other City

Cape Town doesn't have one centre — it has about seven. The Atlantic Seaboard runs from Sea Point down to Camps Bay and Hout Bay. The City Bowl sits beneath Table Mountain. Green Point and De Waterkant are walkable from the V&A Waterfront. Each area has a completely different feel, price point, and reason to be there. Getting your neighbourhood right is the first decision.

The Atlantic Seaboard: Camps Bay, Sea Point, Green Point

This is Cape Town's most iconic strip. Camps Bay has the mountain-meets-beach backdrop that ends up on every Instagram grid. Sea Point is where locals actually live — a long beachside promenade, great restaurants, and hotels that cost significantly less than Camps Bay for a similar experience. Green Point sits between the two and is close to the stadium and Waterfront.

Best for: beach, sunsets, the full Atlantic Seaboard experience. Busiest in December–January. Book early.

The City Bowl and CBD

The CBD has changed dramatically in the last decade. Long Street is the classic — bars, backpackers, boutique hotels in heritage buildings. De Waterkant is quieter and more upmarket. Gardens is residential and walkable to the Company's Garden and the museums on Government Avenue. If you're in Cape Town for business or a short city break, the CBD puts you close to everything without the Atlantic Seaboard price tag.

The V&A Waterfront

Staying at the Waterfront means you're walking distance from the ferry to Robben Island, the Two Oceans Aquarium, the best restaurants in the city, and the Cape Wheel. It's the most convenient base for a first-time Cape Town visit. Prices reflect that — expect to pay more here — but you save on transport and get a lot of atmosphere without trying.

Constantia and the Winelands Edge

If you want to stay outside the city but still be 20–30 minutes from the action, Constantia is worth considering. It's wine farm country right on Cape Town's doorstep — quieter, greener, and with a very different pace from the Atlantic Seaboard. Good for families or couples who want space without driving to Stellenbosch.

When to Visit Cape Town

December and January are peak summer — hot, busy, and expensive. February and March stay warm but quieter, and prices drop. April and May are arguably the best months: the summer crowds have left, the weather is still excellent, and you'll get better availability across the board. Winter (June–August) brings cold Atlantic weather but also the cheapest rates and whale watching season along the coast.

What Does a Hotel in Cape Town Cost?

Budget stays in the CBD or Sea Point start from around R600–R900 per night. Mid-range boutique hotels — the sweet spot for most travellers — run R1,200–R2,500. Camps Bay beachfront and Waterfront hotels go from R2,500 upward, and significantly more in peak December.
