Garden Route Road Trip: Best Hotels Along the Way

By BLY. September 2026 9 min read

The Garden Route is South Africa's most famous road trip — a 300-kilometre stretch of coast and forest between Mossel Bay and the Storms River mouth in Tsitsikamma. Lagoons, cliffs, whale-watching beaches, hiking trails through ancient yellowwood forests, and a string of towns each with a completely different personality. The question isn't whether to do it. It's where to stop and how long to stay.

How to Plan Your Garden Route Trip

The Garden Route works in both directions — east from Cape Town via the N2 or west from Port Elizabeth (now Gqeberha). Most visitors do it in four to seven days, which is enough to stop meaningfully at three or four towns. Rushing it in two days means you'll see the N2 and miss the point entirely. The best approach: pick two or three towns to overnight, and use them as bases for day trips to the smaller spots in between.

On BLY., the Garden Route towns are consistently among our most searched destinations outside Cape Town. Knysna, Plettenberg Bay, and Wilderness tend to book out during school holidays and December — plan ahead.

Stop 1: Mossel Bay — The Western Gateway

📍 Km 0 — Western Start

Mossel Bay

The Garden Route's unofficial starting point is more than a fuel stop. Mossel Bay has one of South Africa's oldest functioning harbours, reliable whale watching (June–November), and the Santos Beach — one of the few north-facing beaches on the south coast, which means it's protected from the prevailing south-westerly and almost always calm. It's a relaxed, working-town vibe rather than a tourist enclave. Stay a night if you're driving in from Cape Town and want to break the journey properly.

Stop 2: George — The Practical Hub

📍 Km 50 — Inland Gateway

George

George has the Garden Route's main airport, which makes it a natural entry point if you're flying in. The town itself is inland — about 9km from the coast — and is the largest in the region. Most visitors pass through rather than overnight here, but George punches above its weight for golf (the Fancourt estate just outside town is world-class) and as a base for accessing Wilderness and the Outeniqua mountains. If you're hiring a car and starting the route, you'll land here first.

Stop 3: Wilderness — Underrated and Beautiful

📍 Km 65 — Best Kept Secret

Wilderness

Wilderness is consistently underestimated. The beach is magnificent — wide, wild, and much less crowded than Knysna or Plett. The Touw River estuary runs directly behind the main beach strip, so you can kayak through reed beds in the morning and be on the Indian Ocean in the afternoon. The Wilderness National Park encompasses a series of lakes connected by the Touw River system — this is one of South Africa's great underexplored wilderness areas. Accommodation here ranges from self-catering beach houses to small boutique guesthouses, and prices are noticeably lower than the bigger names further east.

Stop 4: Knysna — The Garden Route's Capital

📍 Km 100 — The Main Event

Knysna

Knysna is the heart of the Garden Route and the town most visitors spend the most time in. The lagoon — enclosed by the famous Heads, two sandstone cliffs that guard the only entrance to the sea — is one of South Africa's most photographed natural features. The waterfront has great restaurants, the oyster industry is genuinely excellent (the Knysna Oyster Festival in July is one of South Africa's best food events), and the surrounding Knysna Forest offers hiking in some of the last remaining coastal forest in the country. It's busier and more expensive than Wilderness, but earns it. Book well ahead for December and Easter.

Stop 5: Plettenberg Bay — Beach Paradise

📍 Km 140 — Beach Central

Plettenberg Bay

Plett is where Johannesburg goes on holiday. Central Beach and Lookout Beach are among the best swimming beaches on the south coast — warm water (by Cape standards), gentle surf, and the backdrop of the Tsitsikamma mountains behind town. The town itself is more upmarket than Knysna, with good restaurants and a lively summer atmosphere. Outside of December–January, Plett quietens considerably and offers excellent value — the same properties at a fraction of the peak price. Whale and dolphin watching here is outstanding from around May through November.

Stop 6: Nature's Valley — Slow Down Completely

📍 Km 170 — The Quiet One

Nature's Valley

Nature's Valley is one of the smallest and most beautiful settlements on the entire south coast — a handful of holiday houses, a general store, and a beach where the Groot River meets the sea in a lagoon. There are almost no hotels; accommodation is almost entirely private self-catering rentals. But if you can find a place (book months ahead for summer), this is the Garden Route at its most pristine and unhurried. The De Vasselot section of the Garden Route National Park starts right here, and the Otter Trail — South Africa's most famous hiking trail — begins a few kilometres east.

Stop 7: Tsitsikamma — Forest, Cliffs, and the Storms River

📍 Km 200 — Eastern Finale

Tsitsikamma and Storms River

Tsitsikamma is where the Garden Route ends properly — rugged cliffs, ancient forests, and the Storms River suspension bridge over one of the most dramatic gorges on the south coast. The Tsitsikamma National Park is a must: the walk to the suspension bridge takes about 40 minutes from the park's main camp and is one of the most rewarding short hikes in South Africa. The nearby village of Storms River is small but lively, with adventure operators offering bungee jumping from the Bloukrans Bridge (the world's highest commercial bungy at 216m) and canopy tours through the forest. Stay at least one night here — it's a long drive from anywhere else and deserves more than a day-trip.

When to Drive the Garden Route

The Garden Route has no real bad season. Summer (November–February) is warm, busy, and expensive — particularly Knysna and Plett, which fill with Gauteng families on school holiday. Autumn (March–May) is arguably perfect: the summer crowds clear, prices drop, and the weather stays excellent. Winter (June–August) brings whale season to the coast, quieter roads, and very good hotel availability — bring layers for evenings. Spring (September–October) sees the wildflowers bloom along the route and is consistently good value.
