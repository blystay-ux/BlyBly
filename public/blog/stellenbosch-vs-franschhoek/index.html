Stellenbosch vs Franschhoek: Which Winelands Town to Stay In

By BLY. September 2026 6 min read

The Cape Winelands are one of South Africa's great travel experiences — world-class wine, mountain scenery, excellent food, and a pace of life that makes you want to stay longer than you planned. Stellenbosch and Franschhoek are the two towns most visitors choose between, and they're different enough that picking the wrong one for your trip genuinely changes what you get. Here's how to decide.

The Basic Geography

Both towns are less than an hour from Cape Town — Stellenbosch is about 50km east along the N2 and R310, Franschhoek is 75km via the Helshoogte Pass or the Franschhoek Pass from the N1. They're only 30km apart from each other, so whichever town you base yourself in, the other is easily a half-day trip. But your choice of base sets the tone for everything else: the style of accommodation, the type of wine farms you'll access first, the restaurants you'll walk to, and the pace of the days.

Stellenbosch: The Bigger Picture

Stellenbosch is the Winelands' largest town and its undisputed commercial centre. It's a university town — Stellenbosch University is one of South Africa's most prestigious — which gives it an energy and a density of restaurants, coffee shops, and street life that Franschhoek doesn't have. Dorp Street, lined with Cape Dutch architecture and giant oak trees, is one of the most photogenic streets in South Africa. The town square has restaurants and a market atmosphere on weekends. You can genuinely walk from your hotel to dinner, to a wine bar, to a gallery, and back — and that walkability is a significant advantage over most Winelands accommodation, which tends to sit on wine farms with nothing walkable nearby.

The wine farms around Stellenbosch are diverse in every way — size, style, price, and what they produce. The Stellenbosch Wine Routes include over 150 estates, covering the Simonsberg, Helderberg, and Bottelary Hills. You'll find everything from ultra-premium single-vineyard Cabernets to approachable, affordable blends. The sheer range means this is the better base if you want to explore seriously and widely.

Franschhoek: The Food and Wine Capital

Franschhoek occupies a single valley — the Franschhoek Valley — surrounded on three sides by the Drakenstein and Franschhoek mountains. The mountain walls trap the afternoon light in a way that makes the whole valley glow gold at sunset. The town itself is a single main street, Huguenot Road, lined with galleries, wine shops, delicatessens, and some of South Africa's most celebrated restaurants. Le Quartier Français, Pierneef à La Motte, and Haute Cabrière have all built international reputations from this one small valley.

The Franschhoek Wine Tram — a hop-on-hop-off tram service that connects the farms in the valley — is one of the most enjoyable ways to spend a day in the Winelands. You don't need a car, you can drink freely, and the farms the tram stops at are well curated. It's a genuinely excellent experience, not just a tourist gimmick.

What Franschhoek gives up is range. The valley is geographically constrained, the number of farms is much smaller than Stellenbosch, and the town itself can feel slightly static in the evenings — most visitors are at wine estate dinners rather than wandering the main street. It also runs more expensive across the board: accommodation, restaurants, and even tastings tend to cost more here than in Stellenbosch for a comparable experience.

The Honest Comparison: What You Actually Get

  • Stellenbosch is better for: wine explorers, first-timers who want variety, travellers on mixed budgets, anyone who values a proper town with street life.
  • Franschhoek is better for: serious food and wine lovers who want a curated, world-class experience, couples looking for a luxury romantic escape, anyone who has already done Stellenbosch.
  • Stellenbosch wins on: value, walkability, range of options, university-town energy.
  • Franschhoek wins on: restaurant quality, the valley's extraordinary scenery, the Wine Tram, and a more rarefied atmosphere.
  • Both have: exceptional Cape Dutch architecture, mountain backdrops, wine farms with big names, easy access from Cape Town.
The BLY. Verdict

First trip? Stellenbosch. Second trip? Franschhoek.

If you're visiting the Winelands for the first time, Stellenbosch gives you more — more wine farms, more price options, more town energy, and more flexibility. You won't leave feeling like you missed out. If you've been before and want something more concentrated and special — or if food and a few exceptional restaurants matter more than exploration — Franschhoek earns its premium. The ideal trip, of course, does both: base in Stellenbosch, spend one full day driving or Tram-ing through Franschhoek.

Practical Tips for Both Towns

  • January and February are harvest season — the farms are busy, the energy is high, and the tasting rooms have actual wine being made in the background.
  • September and October bring wildflowers to the surrounding fynbos and the lowest accommodation prices before summer.
  • Car hire is almost essential if you're doing serious farm-hopping from Stellenbosch. The Wine Tram makes Franschhoek the exception.
  • Book restaurants ahead — the best places in Franschhoek (and Stellenbosch) fill weeks in advance in summer. Same-day bookings are for the disappointed.
  • The Helshoogte Pass between the two towns is one of the most beautiful short mountain drives in the Cape — worth doing even if you're staying put.
