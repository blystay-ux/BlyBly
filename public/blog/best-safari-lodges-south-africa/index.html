Best Safari Lodges in South Africa: Kruger to Private Reserves

By BLY. November 2026 9 min read

"Safari" covers a lot of ground in South Africa. It can mean a self-drive weekend with a cooler box in the boot, or a game lodge where a ranger and tracker take you off-road to find a leopard before breakfast. Both are great. They just suit different budgets and different trips. This guide sorts the best safari lodges in South Africa by the type of safari you want, from the Greater Kruger to the malaria-free reserves a few hours from Joburg, so you can pick the right one first time.

First, Choose Your Type of Safari

Before you compare lodges, decide which of these three you are after. It changes everything: the price, the drive, the game viewing and whether you need malaria advice.

Greater Kruger: The Private Reserves

The private reserves along Kruger's western boundary share open borders with the national park, so the animals move freely between them. The big difference is how you see them. Lodges here can drive off-road to follow a sighting, the number of vehicles at each sighting is limited, and most stays include all meals and two game drives a day. You pay for that, but this is where most people get their best Big Five sightings.

Malaria-Free Big Five Reserves

Kruger and the Greater Kruger are in a malaria risk area. The risk is lowest in the dry winter months, but many families with small children, and anyone pregnant, prefer to avoid it. Pilanesberg, Madikwe, the Waterberg reserves and the Eastern Cape reserves are malaria-free and still have the Big Five. Many are also a much shorter drive from Gauteng. Speak to a doctor or travel clinic for medical advice before any trip to a malaria area.

The BLY. Verdict

Once-in-a-lifetime? Go private. Every year? Self-drive Kruger.

For a first safari or a big occasion, a Greater Kruger private reserve gives you the best chance of close, unhurried Big Five sightings. If you travel with young kids or only have a long weekend, Pilanesberg or the Waterberg gets you into Big Five country in two to three hours from Gauteng, with no malaria. And if you go every year, a Kruger self-drive stay with a Wild Card is the best value in South African travel.

When to Go on Safari in South Africa

  • May to September (dry winter): thinner bush and animals gathering at water make game viewing easier. Malaria risk in Kruger is at its lowest. Mornings and evenings on open vehicles are cold, so pack layers.
  • October to April (green summer): lush scenery, newborn animals from around November, and great birding. Expect heat, afternoon storms and more mosquitoes.
  • School holidays: Kruger rest camps and family lodges fill up fast in December, Easter and the July holidays. Book early.

Safari Lodges in South Africa: FAQs

What is the best safari area in South Africa?
For Big Five game viewing, the private reserves of the Greater Kruger, such as Sabi Sand, Thornybush and Kapama, are the best known. For malaria-free safaris, Madikwe, Pilanesberg, the Waterberg reserves and the Eastern Cape reserves are excellent alternatives.

Is Kruger National Park malaria-free?
No. Kruger and the Greater Kruger reserves are in a malaria risk area, with the lowest risk in the dry winter months from about May to September. Pilanesberg, Madikwe, the Waterberg and the Eastern Cape reserves are malaria-free. Ask a doctor or travel clinic for advice before you travel.

How much is the Kruger Park entrance fee?
From 1 November 2026 to 31 October 2027, SANParks charges South African citizens and residents R140 per adult per day in Kruger. SADC nationals and international visitors pay higher rates. A SANParks Wild Card can cover conservation fees.

What is the difference between Kruger and a private game reserve?
In Kruger you drive yourself on public roads and must stay on them. In the private reserves on Kruger's western boundary, lodges run guided game drives in open vehicles, can drive off-road to follow animals, and limit the number of vehicles at each sighting.

What is the closest Big Five safari to Johannesburg?
Pilanesberg National Park is the closest Big Five reserve to Johannesburg, at about two and a half hours' drive, next to Sun City. Dinokeng, about 40 minutes from Pretoria, is another close option.

When is the best time for a safari in South Africa?
May to September is the best time for game viewing, because the bush is thinner and animals gather at waterholes. October to April is green and beautiful, with newborn animals and good birding, but hotter and wetter.
